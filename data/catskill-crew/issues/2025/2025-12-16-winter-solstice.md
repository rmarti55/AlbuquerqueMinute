Welcome back my friends, come in and take a seat. Winter is nearly here, I hope you stockpiled your cords of wood and are ready. It was great to see everyone at the Queen of the Catskills premiere! It’s a big one, so let’s get right into it and I’ll see ya out there.

STATUS: SUCCESS

The first Dinner Club is in the books and we had over 100 Crew Members come out to meet some neighbors and have some good times. Friends were made, group chats were spun up, and all signs point to a round 2.

Big thanks to all the restaurants that hosted us and Stockade Tavern for taking us all in for the afterparty. The homemade eggnog was 14 out of 10 stars. Not sure why I hated on eggnog for so long — I’ve become… egg’lightened. I’ll share some dates next week and we can lock in our next adventure.

RESUPPLY

GARDEN REPORT

CONTRIBUTOR: SANDY

Hi folks, well, the ground is frozen here in the Catskills … Like many who garden, I tend to think about buying seeds in December, if I remember to. I personally am not someone who’s gotten into saving seeds — yet. Maybe I will get into seed saving eventually; it’s just a matter of my bandwidth. I often find this whole gardening for “fun” business can take up an immense amount of my energy and time. Hence I try to decide what parts to focus on (or not), the horizon perpetual. 

Years ago, I profiled Ina Garten (humblebrag, I know). I often recall something she said and I was reflecting that I now buy seeds with a similar spirit. I’d asked how she decides what recipe she wants to develop on a given day. In so many words she answered she just cooks what she wants to eat. So if she’s craving a Waldorf salad, she might wind up focusing on that. Similarly, I buy seeds thinking about what I’m going to want to cook — and eat — next spring, summer, or fall. 

Maybe obvious but before buying seeds, consider your own gardening goals — if not culinary, perhaps aesthetic, medicinal, ease of cultivation, etc. Initially, I was sometimes too optimistic in terms of what I might want to grow, buying way too many packets of seeds. I’d wind up having no space for some stuff sometimes, and/or find what I grew didn’t get eaten or harvested in time. Nowadays, I’ve honed my sense of what actually gets most enthusiastically eaten around here — or successfully given away — and what does not. 

I’m a fan of Hudson Valley Seed Co., who saw me link to their site in my columns. They reached out, kindly offering a 15% discount for Catskill Crew readers. (“Use the code CREW15 at checkout. This code is not valid with other offers. For purchase of full price merchandise only. This code will end January 31, 2026.”)

Good luck, seed buyers! My advice: Don’t dawdle! In my experience, exciting new offerings especially tend to sell fast … More next month, Sandy
