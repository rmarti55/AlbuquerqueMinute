I've pulled together a few absolute gems that cover everything from bluestone quarries to Borscht Belt comedians, and Wall Street tycoons to trout streams.

The Catskills: Its History and How It Changed America by Stephen M. Silverman and Raphael D. Silver, tells the wild story of how this mountain region went from a relatively unknown wilderness to become America's first great vacation spot and cultural melting pot.

Kingston: City on the Hudson by Alf Evers is a definitive account of how this small Dutch settlement grew into one of New York’s most historically significant cities. Evers begins with the region's origins as the homeland of the Esopus people, then traces Kingston's evolution.

American Rascal by Greg Steinmetz is a wild ride through the life of Jay Gould, who was basically the Gordon Gekko of his time. Gould started as a surveyor in the Catskills and ending up as one of the most powerful (and hated) guys on Wall Street.

Catskill Rivers by Austin M. Francis is like the bible for anyone who cares about fly fishing - it tells the story of how these mountain streams became the birthplace of American fly fishing as we know it. The book makes you want to grab your rod and head to the river, while also teaching you about the legends who created all those classic fly patterns we still use today.
