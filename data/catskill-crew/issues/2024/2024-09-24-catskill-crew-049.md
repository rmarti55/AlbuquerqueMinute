🎣 Angler Report

I've found myself standing in the Esopus for four nights in a row in pursuit of a very particular fish—specifically, a rainbow trout that I estimate to be 23 inches and has been quite successful in fattening up for the colder temperatures ahead.

It all started when I made my way to one of my favorite spots, tying on a dry fly to match the Isonychia hatch that was underway. On the first cast, I saw a flash under the surface. Holy crap, that’s a big fish, I thought to myself—and very likely muttered out loud. Minutes or maybe an hour passed; time seemed to stop as I emptied my entire fly box, trying to find a pattern that would get this fish to move. Finally, I re-rigged, switching to streamers (for those who don’t fly fish, this is a BIG pattern designed to mimic smaller fish)—a Drunk & Disorderly, to be precise. On the first cast, the fish exploded out of the water, granting me my first good glimpse. It missed the streamer completely, and I swear it laughed and flipped me off with a fin before disappearing.

The next day, I had another opportunity, but the fish bit the tail of the streamer, missing the hook and quickly realizing it was a ruse. The following days proved to be less productive in the pursuit. Sure, I caught other trout each evening, and found some solace standing in the water, but I would have loved the chance to fight that fish... only to release it to watch it swim off.
