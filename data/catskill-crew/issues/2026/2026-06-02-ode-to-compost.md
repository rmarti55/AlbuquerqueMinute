ODE TO MY COMPOST PILE

TOGETHER WITH SANDY — OUR HEAD GARDENER

Hey crewfolk — Since I began this column I’ve wanted to write an ode to my compost pile. Why? Because I love my compost pile. Sometimes we gardening types, we get like this, we become compost-heads; I’m such a cliché. 

I love the compost pile because it really feels like you’re making something with “nothing.” Something valuable even. Buying new nutritious soil is, alas, quite expensive, as new gardeners learn. 

The comedian Zach Galifianakis has a new show on Netflix called This is a Gardening Show (that I highly recommend). It made me both laugh and learn. His final episode focused on one such compost evangelist.

Compost is the food you feed your garden. It’s how you support your vegetables you’re growing especially with sufficient nutrients. Doesn’t matter if you have great seeds, if soil quality is poor, growth suffers.

A good healthy compost comes from a few things: First, a mix of the right ingredients. You’ll hear about “greens” and “browns” and about wanting to use about half and half of each. Examples of the former include (selected) kitchen scraps (no meat / dairy for example), also egg shells and coffee grounds. A quintessential example of the “browns” is leaves. Other ingredients are water (should stay moist), oxygen, and also time. 

You’re going to want to turn your compost periodically. I do this by hand with my shovel, mixing and chopping apart bigger chunks. A happy healthy compost will be rich, dark brown, everything broken down, not smelly, with ideally many worms. 

I used to have a two-sided “E-shaped” wood-pallet bin (note: use untreated wood) that rotted away; my compost setup nowadays is just two side-by-side heaps. Periodically I flip one heap on top of itself onto the other spot. Other gardeners use barrel composters they turn. Consider your own needs and preferences, spatially, abilities, and budget-wise. 

Watching that compost episode, I did think once more about buying an enclosed composter, to prevent critters from getting into my compost (which sure happens here beside the forest). 

As with so many tasks gardening related, it’s on my never-ending to-do list… Sandy
