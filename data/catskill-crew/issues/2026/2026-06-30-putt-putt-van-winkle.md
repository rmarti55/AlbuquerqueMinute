PUTTERING AROUND WITH
SCOTT HILL

CONTRIBUTED BY JACKIE BUDDIE

On any given day, you might find Scott Hill scraping moss off the green, fishing a rainbow of golf balls out of a tangle of weeds, or surveying the folkloric cast of cutouts that adorn the Putt Putt Van Winkle course for any characters who could use a touch of paint—a trio of brushes tucked in his shirt pocket, just in case.

True, this rustic putter’s realm that Scott so dutifully maintains is a product of his own overflowing imagination (plus a lot of plywood), but the 32-par course, which tells the story of Rip Van Winkle over nine whimsically designed holes (think balls that come shooting out of Winkle’s nose), belongs as much to the people as it does to the land from whence it sprang. “I’m sort of obsessed,” Scott says of the donation-based game he hand-built in partnership with long-time friend Brooke Alderson. “I'm determined to do a thing here that works.”

And magically, it does. Nestled along the rocky banks of the Little Delaware between sleepy cow pastures and draped in lush overgrowth, it feels like the type of place that’s always been part of the landscape, and that could only be stumbled upon by mistake. “We let things age in the weather. The way the plants and trees are starting to grow, it's starting to look better and better, like it's been here 50 years and is part of the old Catskills,” Scott explains. “I always loved the idea of traveling and you see some place where it says, ‘Open.’ And you're like, ‘Let's check it out.’ That's my fantasy—to build a place that has this presence, and then have people that help develop the life of it somehow.”

And perhaps that’s the real magic. Post-putt, sidle up to the open air snack bar for some ice cream and a Winklebeer and you just might be served by a Catskills legend like Scott himself—folk artist, wood whittler, mountain mystic—an unmistakable community fixture with a great brambly beard even ol’ Rip would be envious of. “I feel part of it, you know? I love the lure of the mountains, and the lore. Especially here, it's kind of palpable.”
