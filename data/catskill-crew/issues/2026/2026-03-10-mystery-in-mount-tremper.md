THE GREAT MIGRATION

CONTRIBUTED BY IVY MACKEY

As winter rapidly melts into spring, a terrific migration begins. Amphibians — wood frogs, yellow-backed salamanders, northern red salamanders — emerge from their winter burrows with a singular quest: journeying to vernal pools (temporary small pools that dry up by summer) for mating season.

This can involve traversing up to a quarter mile to reach their destination and unfortunately is often punctuated by perilous road crossings. Amphibians are so deeply important to our ecosystem - truly tiny but mighty! They provide insect population regulation, a food source for predators, and nutrient cycling between aquatic and terrestrial environments.

So how can you help protect these tiny beings on their journey? You can assist at high volume crossing locations near you and collect data for future protection and research. Not up for holding little critters? That's okay, you can just avoid driving on those wet 40 degree nights during March and April, especially back roads/roads with waterways.

For more info on training, updates on when migration is occurring, and where to get involved near you, visit the DEC site here.

Thank you for helping protect our tiny neighbors!
