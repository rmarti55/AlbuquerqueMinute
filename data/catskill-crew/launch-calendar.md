GARDEN REPORT

WOODCHUCK WARS

CONTRIBUTED BY SANDY

Hey all — Just wanted to share the tale of my garden this summer versus a woodchuck … 

It all started back in June, just as I’d gotten everything in the ground. Something was eating my main garden, I noticed. Some creature was really destroying particular favorites, it seemed — my peas ripped down and munched on, my cabbages and broccoli and brussels chomped away. Entire squash vines or bean plants, gone. The culprit even preferred particular varietals of kale. Eight-going-on-nine years gardening here I’d never had destruction this bad. 

The fence out there being too tall for deer to clear, my lead suspects were woodchuck or perhaps rabbit. I started doing what I’d previously been advised to in response to woodchucks, e.g. setting a humane trap. I consulted my best friend back in Iowa, who asked her farmer dad. He recommended a side-dressing of blood meal (which apparently “chucks” don’t like), also spraying plants with a concoction of mostly water, essential oils and some drops of dish soap. I did all this. We installed a critter cam, solar-powered. 

Finally: The camera caught a woodchuck in broad daylight, grazing on chard. Leashing my dog, we sprinted out, where to my dog’s profound excitement we caught glimpse of the woodchuck darting away. He then smelled around a long time, a detective casing a crime scene. 

Perhaps we did scare it … Upshot is: I haven’t seen the woodchuck inside the garden since. The damage seems to have stopped.

I admit I was afterwards distracted. We have our peach trees and other younger fruit trees surrounded by a high fence elsewhere on property. Strolling up to check our peach harvest the other week, we realized the fence (strong, metal) had been mangled, clearly by a bear. Our early-ripening, original tree’s ample harvest had been mostly consumed, pits everywhere. 

We commenced to guarding our peach tree until we could harvest the rest, which did seem to work this time. That fence however will need professional help. Never ends! 

So it goes, gardening amongst wilderness. Can’t control much, except our own responses. Happy harvesting — Sandy

Come on out for a good ol’ fashioned BBQ & Block Party in Woodstock with Woodstock Meats and The Clubhouse.

We’ll be grillin’ smash burgs, Latin-inspired chicken sandos, house-made sausages, and so much more. We’ll be slingin’ beer from Kingston Standard, Hugo spritzes, hibiscus margs, and a watermelon vodka fizz — just to name a few.

If you've heard Brittany Howard sing, you know. And Mavis? She's been singing since the 1950s — and she's still got it. All of it outdoors, on the same grounds where Woodstock happened in '69.

THE RIVER:

A new documentary, The River, is making its grand premiere.

The film follows Dan Plummer and the crew over at Friends of the Upper Delaware River — featuring locals who show up day after day, year after year, to protect this wild and beautiful river. It's a story about friendship, stubbornness, and the "River family" that forms around a shared stretch of water.

The River premieres at Tinker Street Cinema in Woodstock on Saturday, September 5th at 7PM, and Callicoon Theatre on Friday, September 4th at 7:30PM.

Grab a ticket here or at the door.

TOMATO FEST:

Tomato season is peaking — and Hudson Valley Seed Co is throwing their second annual Tomato Fest at the farm in Accord on Saturday, September 5th from 1 to 4PM.

The lineup runs deep: Heirloom tastings, cooking demos, pizza from Itsa Pizza Truck, seed-processing demos, live screen printing, pick-your-own dahlias, and a book talk. Bring an appetite and get the details here.
